# Spider Goats
Spider goats are genetically modified goats that have been engineered to produce spider
silk proteins in their milk. Scientists achieved this by introducing genes from a golden
orb-weaver spider into the goat's DNA, specifically targeting expression in their mammary
glands so that the silk protein is secreted along with the milk. The reason for this groundbreaking
work was to find an efficient way to mass-produce spider silk, known for its exceptional
strength, elasticity, and lightweight properties, which holds immense potential for applications
in medicine, textiles, and defense. While the goats generally appear healthy, concerns
exist regarding the ethicalness of genetically modifying animals, potential health 
effects, and the acceptance of consuming products derived from genetically-engineered
organisms. The artificial spider silk produced by these goats is often referred to as
BioSteel. This boasts an extreem strength comparable to steel, yet maintains an
elasticity similar to nylon.

# Narlugas
A narluga is a rare HYBRID sea mammal resulting from the natural interbreeding of a
narwhal and a beluga whale. Unlike the spider goat, this animal was not created through
human genetic stuff like genetic engineering or selective breeding; it is a product of
natural cross-species mating. The occurrence of narlugas is not a result of human intention,
but rather a rare phenomenon that may be influenced by factors such as overlapping 
habitats due to climate change or changes in species-specific mate availability. While
there are no direct concerns about the existence of the narluga itself as a natural
occurrence, its extreme rarity and the potential environmental factors contributing
to its existence could indirectly raise broader ecological questions about the health
of Arctic marine ecosystems and the populations of its parent species.
Only one confirmed skull of a narluga has ever been discovered and studied, found
in West Greenland in 1990. This unique specimen exhibited a peculiar dental structure,
displaying teeth intermediate between the narwhal's single tusk and the beluga's characteristic
dentition.

# Pink Pineapple
The "Pink Pineapple," specifically Del Monte's "Pinkglow" variety, is a genetically engineered fruit distinguished by its vibrant pink flesh, a crazy remake of the traditional yellow. This unique color was achieved through genetic engineering, where the pineapple was geneticly modified to suppress the enzymes that typically convert lycopene, the pigment responsible for red and pink hues in fruits like watermelon; into “beta-carotene”, allowing the lycopene to accumulate. The why in this development was market differentiation and consumer appeal, aiming to offer a visually novel and premium fruit experience. While regulatory bodies like the FDA have allowed it as “safe” for eating, broader public concerns often associated with GMOs can arise, including questions about long-term health effects or environmental impact, tho no specific, unique hazards have been scientifically linked to the Pink Pineapple itself.
